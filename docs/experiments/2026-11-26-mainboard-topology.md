# 2026-11-26 Mainboard topology from teardown frames

The pin map below applies to the filmed Vangoa donor with the DC02 Pro touch board and main-board suffix `_MAIN_V1.1_20221115`. USB and sound measurements elsewhere in MIDIEWI apply to the closed original-style DC02. A shared circuit or part between them is a hypothesis. [Donor attribution](2026-11-26-vangoa-video-and-konix-fcc.md)

## Land pattern and orientation

In the native 1920 × 1080 frame at 331 s, the U6 silkscreen triangle is below the lowest right-edge lead. A small package dot in the same corner corroborates the triangle. The larger round molding recesses occur in multiple corners. Viewed with the triangle at lower right, assign **1→12 up the right edge, 13→24 across the top from right to left, 25→36 down the left edge, 37→48 across the bottom from left to right**. When reviewing a different frame, first align the triangle, U6 legend and C53/C60 positions. [U6 frame](https://youtu.be/2Avfv2L7Kl8?t=331)

The approximately 500-pixel body width divided by the approximately 38-pixel center-to-center lead spacing is about 13–14 pitches, consistent with a nominal 7 mm body and 0.50 mm pitch LQFP48 (9 mm nominal lead span for the ST comparator). There is no calibrated dimension in that plane. Treat the ratio and lead count as measured from the picture, and the millimeter dimensions as a package hypothesis. Thickness and the land pattern are unresolved. [ST package drawing](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stm32f103cb.pdf)

The dependency graph supported by the frames is narrow. Tube → SPS is direct. Touch PCB ↔ main PCB via flex is direct. USB1 → local fanout/vias and U6 → local fanout are visible independently. Interconnects through unknown layers, component pads under bodies, and between obscured camera passes remain open. The original DC02 donor still lacks a comparable photograph of its U6-equivalent region.

## LMV324B analog section

In the 345.3 s close-up with the part legend upright, the lower-left triangle and dot put pins 1–7 along the bottom left to right, and 14–8 along the top left to right. The datasheet pinout places outputs on 1, 7, 8 and 14, positive supply on 4 and negative supply on 11. Visible resistor markings include `101` (100 Ω), `103` (10 kΩ), `393` (39 kΩ) and `512` (5.1 kΩ). At least two similar resistor groups flank U2. On the GAS side the GAS pad's visible trace appears to head toward the R19/R28 region, then is obscured. The repeated groups could condition audio channels or multiple sensor/reference signals. No defensible closed feedback path, gain, bias voltage, channel assignment or passband can be reconstructed from these views. The small-signal quad op-amp could buffer line audio or sensor voltage, while the speaker-current stage still requires independent identification. [Board close-up](https://youtu.be/2Avfv2L7Kl8?t=345) · [LMV324B datasheet](https://static.3peak.com/res/doc/ds/Datasheet_LMV321B-LMV358B-LMV324B.pdf)

## Descriptor provenance

The original-style unit's Dream VID/PID is positive evidence of the **USB identity presented by the firmware**. Dream's own USB descriptor editor and reference configurations demonstrate that descriptor fields are project data. A cross-platform example in TinyUSB demonstrates that a firmware project can supply VID, PID and strings independently of its controller. Possible arrangements include a Dream USB/synthesis IC, a non-Dream USB host-facing MCU with a separate synth, or non-Dream silicon implementing the identity in firmware. The latter two require evidence of either a separate synth or custom software and identity provenance. No specific license, OEM agreement, copied stack, silicon ROM fingerprint, or connection from USB1 to U6 was established. MIDI class structures and endpoint types alone are standardized and are weak fingerprints. The prior comparison found different Dream examples reusing the same pair, with different interface sets.
